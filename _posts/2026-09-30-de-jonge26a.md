abstract: Dating apps are important intermediaries in people finding romance, yet
  dating algorithms are likely to discriminate on basis of ethnicity. Dating apps
  cannot sufficiently measure or mitigate discrimination, as the GDPR prevents dating
  apps from accessing or processing ethnicity. In this work, we show that popularity
  bias can be a driver for discrimination, and that mitigating popularity bias can
  result in reduced algorithmic discrimination in content-based recommendation. Interventions
  on popularity bias appear more effective in content-based recommendation, with an
  appropriately chosen definition of popularity bias, and targeting indirect discrimination,
  rather than direct discrimination.
